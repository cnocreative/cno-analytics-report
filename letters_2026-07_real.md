## 1. Endowe
*Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn — 440 words, 6 figures*

Dear Endowe,

July is your first full month of reporting with us, so there is no earlier month to set it against. What follows is the baseline that everything after this will be measured from.

The month had a clear shape. The first three weeks were steady and quiet across all four channels. Then the fourth week changed the picture entirely: every platform lifted at once, and most of what July produced happened inside that week.

TikTok carried the month. It brought in 4,188 views and 1,547 interactions, and nearly all of that came from a single video in that fourth week. One piece of content doing that much work is worth understanding rather than celebrating. It tells us the audience is there and reachable; it does not yet tell us the result repeats. We will be watching whether the videos that follow hold any of that attention.

Instagram was the steadier performer. It reached 2,887 accounts and drew 183 profile visits, meaning people saw something and then went looking for more. Profile visits are the figure we watch most closely on Instagram, because they sit nearest to someone deciding to get in touch. Instagram also finished the month with more followers than it started with.

Facebook is the surprise. The page itself has almost no following, yet the reels published there reached 2,262 accounts. That reach is coming from Facebook's own distribution rather than from people who follow the page, which is why the follower count and the reach figure look so at odds. The reach is real and it costs nothing extra to keep feeding, but it is not yet turning into engagement.

LinkedIn is small and behaving as a young company page should. It drew 114 profile visits against a modest following, a high rate of curiosity for the size of that audience. It is the channel where the audience is smallest and the intent is highest.

What we are doing next. We are treating the fourth week as a signal to test rather than a result to repeat, so the format and subject that performed on TikTok will run again in a few variations and we will see whether it was the idea or the timing. On Instagram we will keep the pressure on profile visits. On Facebook we will keep publishing the reel format that is earning distribution. On LinkedIn we will hold the cadence steady and let the page build.

One honest caveat: a single month cannot separate a trend from a good week. Next month's report will be the first able to show direction rather than a starting point.

With care,
CNO Creative Co

## 2. Femme Flor
*Instagram, LinkedIn, YouTube — 365 words, 5 figures*

Dear Femme Flor,

July is your first full month of reporting with us, so there is nothing earlier to measure it against. Treat this as the starting line rather than a verdict.

Instagram had a genuinely strong month. It reached 16,847 accounts and added 137 followers, and both of those moved almost entirely in the final full week of July rather than building steadily across it.

The other figure worth sitting with is 860 profile visits. Reach tells you how many people saw something. Profile visits tell you how many cared enough to go and look at who you are, which is the step closest to an enquiry or a booking. That number moved in step with the reach, which is the pattern you want to see.

Two reels did most of the heavy lifting on reach, and both outperformed everything else published that month by a wide margin. Reels are clearly the format carrying this account at the moment.

One honest note about the figures. The account-level totals cover everything happening on the profile, not only the posts published this month, so stories and other surfaces are included too. The reach and views figures are therefore larger than what the twelve posts alone produced. Both are real; they simply measure different things, and we would rather say so plainly than leave the gap unexplained.

LinkedIn and YouTube are both early. LinkedIn reached 34 accounts and YouTube drew 21 views. At these volumes neither has an audience yet, and neither figure should be read as a signal in either direction. They will start to mean something once there is more published behind them.

What we are doing next. Instagram is where the momentum is, so that is where the effort goes: more of the format that worked, at the same cadence, with profile visits as the figure we hold ourselves to. LinkedIn gets a steady drumbeat rather than a push, and YouTube stays as it is for now.

Next month's report will be the first that can show a direction rather than a starting point, and the question it answers is whether that final week was a moment or a beginning.

With care,
CNO Creative Co

## 3. You Matter Beauty
*Facebook, Instagram — 286 words, 4 figures*

> This is the bluntest of the four. It reports a follower decline and puts the low publishing
> volume on CNO. Worth reading closely before it goes out.

Dear You Matter Beauty,

July is your first month of reporting with us, so this is a baseline rather than a comparison. It was a quiet month, and we would rather say that plainly than dress it up.

Instagram reached 1,011 accounts and drew 145 profile visits. Reach was strongest in the first half of July and tapered through the second half. The account also finished the month with 11 fewer followers than it started with. At this size a small loss is closer to normal movement than to a signal, but it is not a direction we want repeated, and it is the first figure we will be watching next month.

The clearest reason for a quiet month is volume: four posts went out in July. That is on us rather than on you, and the fix is straightforward. We are lifting the publishing cadence, and the first thing that should respond is reach, with profile visits following it.

One post did noticeably better than the rest, a reel, which also drew the most comments of anything published that month. Reels are where the response is, so that is where the additional volume will go.

Facebook is effectively dormant. It reached 8 accounts in July and recorded no interactions at all. Nothing is wrong with the page; there is simply very little being published to it and the audience there is not currently active. Our suggestion is to treat Facebook as a low priority and concentrate the effort on Instagram, unless you would rather we keep it running.

What we are doing next: more posts, weighted toward reels, with reach and profile visits as the two figures we hold ourselves to.

With care,
CNO Creative Co

## 4. Adaeze
*Instagram — 202 words, 3 figures*

> The most sensitive of the four: one post went out all month, and the letter says so and
> attributes it to CNO rather than to her.

Dear Adaeze,

July is your first month of reporting with us, so this is a starting point rather than a result.

One post went out during the month. That is the honest headline, and it sits with us rather than with you. With a single piece of content, the figures below describe how that one post travelled far more than they describe how the account is doing.

What did happen: Instagram reached 350 accounts, drew 29 profile visits, and the account gained 5 followers across the month. Almost all of the reach landed in the first week, which is exactly what you would expect when everything rests on one publication date.

The post itself did its job. It was a reel, and on its own it accounted for most of the month's reach. That suggests the format and the audience are fine; there simply was not enough published for anything to build on.

So what we are changing is simple: cadence. Publishing consistently is the single most useful lever here, and until that changes there is little in these numbers worth reading as a trend in either direction. Next month should give us something real to talk about.

With care,
CNO Creative Co
